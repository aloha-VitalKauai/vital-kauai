Ceremony · Moment-by-Moment Guide

Ceremony Day Guide

A moment-by-moment orientation for your ceremony day. What to expect from arrival through the full arc of the night and morning after. Your journey will last between 12 and 36 hours.

Let this guide orient you, then release it, and trust what unfolds.

The Morning

Rising Into Ceremony

The day begins gently. Everything has been prepared. The morning is for settling, quieting, and arriving in the body.

On waking

Breathwork and Gentle Movement

Wake slowly. Take a warm shower or walk on the bay. The morning includes guided breathwork and yoga to settle the nervous system, open the body, and bring you into presence.

Journaling is welcome.

Morning

Physical Nourishment

Light, easily digestible food if your body asks for it: banana, eggs, coconut water, electrolyte drinks, and/or fresh ginger root tea. Food and drink suspend at least six hours before ceremony begins.

Hydrate through the morning. Rest when you feel tired.

Midday

Quiet Preparation

Step away from screens and from conversations that pull you outward. Turn inward. Review your intentions. Read back through your "Questions for the Medicine."

Whatever you are feeling, whether it's excitement, fear, tenderness, or uncertainty, is part of the preparation.

Afternoon

Body Support

Treatment such as reiki, massage, cranio-sacral, or acupuncture.

Late Afternoon

Opening the Container

Before the fire

Dress in White

When it is time, dress in all white, the traditional color of ceremony, clarity, and openness. Let the act of dressing be part of the ritual.

Late afternoon

Opening & Fire Ceremony

Arrive at the fire. Speak what you are releasing, your intentions, and your prayers. The fire receives what is ready to be let go.

Your Four Anchors in Ceremony

What to Remember When You Are Inside It

Be with what arises. These four anchors are always available to you and will carry you through any intensity, any depth, any wave.

  • Your breath is your home. When sensation rises, return to your breath. Slow, full inhale. Slow exhale with sound. One breath is enough to find your way back.
  • Your body is wise. Trust what it asks for, whether stillness, movement, sound, or tears. If it wants to shake, let it shake. If it wants to weep, let it weep. The body is doing its work.
  • Our team is here. If you need support, raise your hand and someone will come to you. You can also speak. Words like "I need help," "I want to be held," "I need water," or "I need space" are enough. We will meet you with whatever you need.
  • It is okay to let go. The temptation in intensity is to push or control. Soften instead. Surrender to what is unfolding.

The Journey

The Arc of the Night

Your journey can last between 12 and 36 hours. What follows is a map of the territory many travelers move through. Trust what unfolds.

Hours 1–3

Onset

Return to the ceremony space, which has been prepared for you. Dosing begins here, titrated gradually over the first several hours of your journey. Your ceremony guides open the space with invocation, creating a protected, sacred container for what is to come.

Bwiti music begins. You are invited to move with it however feels true—rattle, dance, shake, sound, tone, sing, or remain in stillness. We encourage having your rattle close at all times and using it often.

As onset arrives, you may be guided to lie down and receive an eye mask. Body heaviness, buzzing, tingling, dizziness, vibration, and the beginning of visual patterns are signs the medicine is moving. Your facilitators are present throughout, attentive and attuned to the whole room and to you.

Hours 4–8

The Deepest Hours

Often the most immersive portion of the journey. This can involve a vivid life review, archetypal visions, guidance and insight, and dream-like sequences. Many people remain eyes-closed and turned inward here. Your facilitators offer reassurance, grounding touch when welcomed, support, and simple presence.

You may hear voices or whispers, encounter presences, or sense that vision is possible even behind the eye mask. Iboga opens dimensions of experience that can be vast and unexpected. Whatever arises, you are safe and your facilitators are with you. If you feel called to open your eyes, you may do so gently. Know that you may experience visual distortions when you open them, and this is part of the medicine working.

Hours 8–20

Psycho-Spiritual Processing

This is where meaning-making often begins—life narrative integration, inner child work, ancestral healing, and belief system processing. This is a quiet time of inner insight and processing.

Speak when you feel called to speak or remain silent when silence feels right.

After 20 hrs

Emergence & Grounding

The ceremony moves into its closing arc. Sleep is the greatest medicine available to you at this point. You may feel physically tired and emotionally wide open. This tenderness is sacred and we hold it with full care.

Move slowly and accept assistance as you orient. Dizziness, visual trails, and shifts in perception are a natural part of emergence. The medicine is still working. Light will feel brighter than usual, and you may want to wear sunglasses.

Practical Support

What to Have With You

Almost everything you need is provided. These are the personal items that support your journey and let you settle in fully.

On your body

  • All white, comfortable clothing
  • A light layer for warmth
  • Socks (the body cools during ceremony)

Near your space

  • A water bottle
  • Your eye covering, if you like one

For the heart

  • Photos of loved ones, if it supports you
  • A written intention or prayer
  • Any sacred object that holds meaning for you

For after

  • A journal and pen
  • Comfortable clothes for the morning
  • Sunglasses for the first day after

The Morning After

The Day Beyond

When you emerge, the world will meet you softly. The medicine is still working within you, and your system is still settling. This is a tender, open, sacred time, a gentle passage from ceremony into the first movements of integration.

What the Morning Asks of You

Rest is the work of this morning. You will most likely still be lying down, processing, and this is exactly right. Eat gently when nourishment is welcome: fresh fruit, broth, something warm and easy. Sip water slowly. Let the body stay horizontal as long as it asks to.

If you feel called to move later in the afternoon, follow what your body allows. The ordinary world can wait.

You may feel wide open, emotionally sensitive, physically tired, or surprisingly clear. All of these are part of emergence. There is no right way to be the morning after.

Our team will be with you, checking in, offering nourishment and presence. Journaling can be powerful here, simply to capture what arose and what is arriving now. You will be guided into your integration support in the days ahead.

Part Two · Foundational Reading

Safety in the Body

How to orient within intense somatic experience during ceremony. What you may feel, how to work with it, and what our team is here to support.

The deepest medicine arrives through the body. Sensation, even intense sensation, is the path itself. Meeting it is the work.

What You May Feel

The Language of the Body

Sensation will move, familiar and new, gentle and intense. All of it belongs. What follows is a partial map; every sensation on it is a sign your system is doing exactly what it was built to do.

Physical

Heaviness, vibration, heat or cold, tingling

Your body may feel like it weighs a great deal, or like it is vibrating from the inside. Waves of warmth or cold can move through you. Limbs may feel electric or slightly numb. These are all common sensations as the nervous system shifts state and the body opens.

Involuntary

Shaking, trembling, yawning, tears, sound

The body knows how to discharge. Trembling, shaking, deep yawns, spontaneous tears, and unexpected sounds are how tension and held experience move out. Let them. What wants to move will move more easily when you stop trying to hold it still.

Emotional

Waves of grief, joy, fear, love, longing

Emotions often arrive in waves rather than as thoughts. You may weep without a story attached. You may laugh without reason. You may feel a deep tenderness, or a fierce grief, or a pure and inexplicable joy. Let each wave rise and pass. Honor it simply by letting it move through.

Perceptual

Time softening, imagery, visions, inner sound

In Iboga ceremony, perception shifts. Time may stretch or fold. Imagery, memories, archetypal visions, and inner sounds may arise. Some moments will feel luminous and clear; others will feel dreamlike and strange. Let each one come as it comes, and let it pass when it is ready.

Mental

The fight-flight-freeze voice

Your thinking mind may try to keep you safe in the way it always has, by narrating, controlling, analyzing, doubting, or wanting to leave. This is the survival part of you doing its job. Let it speak, thank it for its care, and return to your breath. The deeper wisdom will take over from there.

Working With Intensity

Five Principles for Meeting What Arises

You are safe. You are held by our team, by the lineage, by the medicine itself, and by the land of Kauaʻi. Whatever arises is the medicine working, and the wave will pass. Understanding can wait. Whatever you are doing is exactly it. Whenever the body feels overwhelming, one of these five will meet you where you are.

One

When in doubt, do less

The temptation in intensity is to try harder, push through, or fix. The body is already doing its work and asks only for space. Soften the jaw, soften the belly, let the shoulders drop. Do less and let the medicine do more.

Two

Move with what is moving

If the body wants to shake, let it shake. If sound wants to come, let it come. If breath wants to deepen or quicken, follow it. Trauma and held experience move through us with breath, sound, and movement. Allowing the movement is how you pass through. The movement itself is the medicine.

Three

Return to the breath

Your breath is the most available anchor you have. It is always with you, steady, simple, yours. When sensation becomes disorienting, find one slow inhale. Then one slow exhale. One breath is enough. The next one will come.

Four

Practice alone together

You are in a shared container with other members, and you are on your own sacred thread. Stay with your own experience. Keep your attention close to your body and trust that your support team is holding the whole room. Your journey is yours. Theirs is theirs.

Five

Trust the wisdom of your body

Your body has carried you through every experience of your life. It knows how to open, how to close, how to discharge, how to rest. Its wisdom runs deeper than the mind can reach. When the mind is uncertain, ask the body. It will answer in sensation, in impulse, in a sense of yes or no. Listen.

Everything we invite is a suggestion and a guide. The deepest instruction always comes from the wisdom of your own body.

You Are Held

Held by Your Team

Our facilitators are trained to read the body, attune to the room, and meet you with what you need. You also have agency, you can communicate at any time, in simple ways.

Simple Signals

  • Raised hand Raise your hand if you need anything: the bathroom, water, to step outside, a facilitator's presence, grounding touch, a cool cloth, or reassurance. Someone will come to you.
  • Words You are welcome to speak. "I need support," "I want to be held," "I need space," "I am overwhelmed." Simple words are enough.
  • Silence If you are turned inward and the work is moving through you, stay with it. We will check on you gently and honor the space you are in.

Throughout ceremony, your team offers all of this

  • Continuous attentive presence: someone is always watching the room with care
  • Grounding touch when welcomed: a hand on the shoulder, the feet, the crown
  • Somatic guidance: invitations to breathe, to sound, to move, or simply to be still
  • Intervention if needed: in the rare moments when something medical or acute arises, we respond immediately and with full skill

Your only task is to be present with what arises in you. We are holding the rest.
